So, I'm doing a blog now.

<!-- excerpt -->

If you know me enough, it may come as a surprise that I decide to do something
like this despite my lack of interest in long format reading or any such kind of
media.

Truth be told, I'm a big stinky liar.

My discontent with the medium comes almost exclusively from my disdain for the
ready made tools that I previously had to my disposal.

Writing a thread on a Twitter-like social media feels like strangling my
thoughts to fit the arbitrary character limit, using some ready made blogging
tool feels annoying since it doesn't usually work with my editor of choice, or
any other number of issues, such as FurAffinity's abhorrent formatting tools.

Markdown has been around for ages, is it *that* hard to make a blog that I can
expressively format all from the editor I want to use and serve into a place I
control, and has all the bells and whistles that I want? Could such a
thing exist?

Yes, it does.

It's the one I make, for myself.

I am once again reminded of sayings like "the world is your oyster" and, my
personal favorite:

> "You can just make things" — Some very smart person, probably

So, thanks to the power of [11ty](https://11ty.dev), to which I ported my site
not too long ago, I decided to just do the deed and give myself an outlet where
I can truly yap.

## So, what is there to expect from this?

For starters, a free newsletter for my thoughts and musings in the form of an
RSS feed (Atom, actually). The link is on that funky little button at the bottom
right of your screen, you should totally click it and add it to your reader of
choice, even if just for the cool animation I made for it.

More seriously, I wanted to carve out a space on the internet where I could hang
up my thoughts for others to see. Given the state of the world turning very
quickly to be at the mercy of algorithms, I wanted a place to not deal with
them.

Finally, and more importantly in my opinion, I wanted to encourage people to just
make things. Because that's the best way to expand one's toolset, and to take
control back from what social media and other algorithm-driven content delivery
have subjected us to. A constant rush of dopamine and anxiety ever burning down
out brains.

I don't like being doom and gloom about these things. I like to think of a
better way to go about, and in this case we had the answer all along with us.

Back in the olden days of the internet, nonsense blogs like the one you're
reading currently were the norm. I wasn't personally around to see them, but
without them we would not have an internet like the one we have today, for
better or worse. I find myself craving these smaller, more personal sites,
seeing someone's personal brain dump is always an amusing thing to read when
having nothing better to do.

I think it should most certainly be brought back, in a sense, especially as it's
not a particularly complex problem to solve with current tooling. Having the
chance to cut away from the networks draining the soul out of everything and, for
a moment, making something you are truly proud of, something where **you** would
like to stay.

## The point

Please take this to heart.

Don't be afraid to make things, don't be afraid to fail, abandon, or rebuilding a 
project, don't be afraid to not be the best, don't be afraid of not knowing how to 
do something.
That is what will hold you from just making things, whatever you may want to make.

There is a certain joy in creativity and expression that is unknown to many, out
of reach by a fear of inadequacy, and to that I say the following:

Just make things.
